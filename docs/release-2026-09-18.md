# What changed — 18 September 2026

Written to be read out. Each item says what it does, then why it was worth
doing. Nothing here needs anybody to learn a new screen.

---

## The register remembers everyone

**Children who have left still appear on the weeks they were here.**

Before, the moment a child was marked Inactive they disappeared from every week
in the system — including the months they attended every day. The records were
never deleted; the register simply refused to draw them.

Now a child is on a week if any of these is true:

- they are Active — the roll as it stands;
- they signed in that week, whatever their record says now;
- they left on or after that Monday and had started by the Friday.

So a child who left in June is on June's weeks and not on September's. A child
marked Inactive with no leaving date recorded appears only on the weeks they
actually signed in — "not with us" and no date is not a statement about any
particular week.

The printed sheet was corrected to match. Paper and screen now list the same
children for the same week.

**Why.** A register is a record as much as it is a worksheet. If it cannot be
looked back at, it is not one — and the centre has to be able to answer
questions about a child months after they leave.

## Active, Pending, Inactive

**A third status: Pending.** A place agreed but not started. They are on the
roll and can be found and filled in, and they are **not** counted as active, so
the rooms are not staffed for them and nobody looks for them at sign-in.

Before this, such a child had to be filed as Active — counted as here — or
Inactive, which reads as having left.

**The status shows under the child's name on the register**, but only when it is
not "Active". Sixty rows all reading *Active* would hide the one that does not.
Shown only where it differs, it is the thing that catches the eye rather than
the thing the eye learns to skip.

**On the children list, chips filter by status:**

> `All 63`  `Active 60`  `Pending 1`  `Inactive 2`

Press one to see only those children. The counts are always the whole roll, so
they do not move when a chip is pressed — they are how the filter is chosen.
A status nobody is in gets no chip; a control that does nothing is worse than no
control.

The filter survives a refresh and can be sent to somebody as a link, and sorting
a column keeps it.

## A child's history on the roll

**Every change of status is now recorded**: what it changed from, what to, the
date, and who made it. On the child's record:

> **Inactive since** 9/18/2026
>
> **On the roll**
> Active → Inactive — 9/18/2026, by Administrator
> Added as Active — 1/5/2026

**Why, when the record already has "Enrolled" and "Withdrawn".** Those two are
boxes on a form: they say what was *intended*, they are only right if somebody
remembered to fill them in, and they hold one answer each. A child who leaves in
June and comes back in September has one withdrawal date and no way to say they
returned. The history is what *happened*, every time it happened, recorded
whether anybody remembered or not.

It is written by the system itself, so it catches a change made on the edit
screen, by an import, or from the command line — not only the one road somebody
thought to instrument.

Nothing here can be edited or deleted. A history that can be rewritten is not
one. Children already on the roll start their history at their next change.

## What the dotted boxes mean

Dotted used to mean two unrelated things on the attendance sheet, which is why
it read as inconsistent.

**It now means exactly one thing: expected, not arrived.**

| Box | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Dotted, empty, blue | Scheduled — expected, not signed in yet |
| Solid, with a time | Signed in |
| Solid amber, with a time | Signed in but not scheduled — still billable |
| Plain grey | A day that cannot be changed |

Two things stopped being dotted:

- **A time corrected by hand.** The correction is still recorded — hover the box
  and it says who changed it and when — but it no longer changes how the box
  looks. Dotted was saying "unfinished" about a record that is finished.
- **The box you type into in Edit mode.** The caret and the green already say it
  is being edited.

**Also:** the grey outline of a finished day was darkened. At the old shade a
locked box read as the *absence* of a box, so a row of them looked like nothing
had been set.

## Alerts on a child

A child's record can now carry short alerts, each with a kind, shown as coloured
chips on the children list and at the top of their record:

| Kind | Colour | For |
|---|---|---|
| Court Order | red | a rule that must not be broken |
| Allergy | amber | a thing to watch |
| Medical | blue | a matter of fact |
| Note | grey | a remark |

The colour is what makes a court order noticeable without being read, which is
the whole point of putting these on the list.

The existing "Allergies, medication, court orders" box stays — an allergy needs
more said about it than fits on a chip, and that is where the detail goes. The
chips are the version that can be read at a glance down a roll of sixty.

## Numbering: LAN

**LANs are now five digits**, issued by the system, counting from 10001.

**They can no longer be typed.** The field is read-only and the number is
assigned on save. Before, it was a text box holding a suggestion: two people
adding children at the same moment both took the number they were shown, and the
second was refused the whole finished form after filling it in.

**A child's page is addressed by their LAN** — `/children/10063` rather than an
internal row number. The LAN is how the paper file and the staff name a child,
so a link can now be read out and checked against the cabinet.

> **For the office:** the existing children were renumbered in their current
> order, so the oldest number is still the oldest child. The old and new numbers
> were written to a file the office can re-key the paper files from. Ask for
> `lan-renumber.csv`.

## Reading the sheet

- **Times read the same everywhere** — `11:54a`, `12:54p` — whole day or half
  day. A whole day used to read `11:52 AM` while the two halves beside it read
  `11:54a`, so comparing two arrivals meant measuring two shapes.
- **Contracted hours stack**, one time a line, so the drop-offs make a column
  and the pick-ups make another.
- **The contracted hours print beside the name**, written the way paper writes
  them: `730-430`. The register could not previously answer "when is this one
  due out?" without leaving the clipboard.
- **The dates in the toolbar open a month.** Hovering picks a whole week, not a
  day, and the footer names the week before you press.
- **Bigger text on bigger screens** — up to about 135% on a desk monitor. If
  anybody has been using the browser's zoom, set it back to 100%: this replaces
  it rather than stacking with it.
- **The key is behind the "?" in the top bar**, and starts out of the way. It is
  read on the first morning and never again.
- **"Back" on a child's record** returns where you came from rather than always
  to the roster — the record is opened from the register too.
